Act as a strategic problem-solving advisor and use the First Principles mental model to analyze the following work problem from the ground up: [Insert situation]. Break the problem down into its most basic, undeniable truths instead of relying on assumptions, industry norms, past habits, or “this is how it’s usually done” thinking. Start by clearly defining the core problem, the desired outcome, and why this problem matters. Then identify every assumption being made about the situation, including assumptions about people, processes, resources, timing, constraints, customers, costs, tools, and expected outcomes. For each assumption, challenge whether it is actually true, partially true, unproven, outdated, or based on convenience. Rewrite each assumption into a verifiable truth or testable question. Once the fundamentals are clear, reconstruct the best possible solution from scratch using only those truths. Propose a redesigned approach that removes unnecessary complexity, focuses on the real constraint, and creates the most efficient path to the desired result. Provide a clear step-by-step solution path, explain the reasoning behind each step, highlight the key risks or trade-offs, and recommend an execution plan with priorities, owners, timelines, success metrics, and next actions. Keep the analysis practical, direct, and focused on what can actually be done.
